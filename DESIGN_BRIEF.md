# The Midnight Theatre — Digital Build Brief

You are building a fully playable online version of "The Midnight Theatre" (working title "The Majestic"), a circus-themed drafting/collection board game for 2-5 players. This brief is your ground truth — implement from this document plus `assets/card_database.json` and the card images in `assets/cards/`. The original rules PDF is also included at `assets/Rules.pdf` for reference, but this brief supersedes it wherever they conflict, since it fills in gaps the PDF leaves open.

## Requirements (non-negotiable)

1. **Online multiplayer with real human players**, not just local hotseat. Different people on different devices/browsers need to be able to join the same game via a room code or link.
2. **AI players** that can fill any empty seat and play a complete, rules-legal game start to finish without a human.
3. **Deployable to a public URL.** I (the coding agent) cannot create accounts on hosting platforms on the owner's behalf, so structure the project to deploy with minimal manual steps: a Node.js backend + static frontend that runs with one `npm install && npm run build && npm start`, plus config for a simple one-click host (Render, Railway, or Fly.io — pick one and include the config file). Document the exact manual steps (account creation, connecting the repo, clicking deploy) in `README.md` since those steps require the owner's own account.
4. Also make sure it **runs locally** (`npm run dev` or equivalent) so it can be tested immediately without any deployment step.

## Suggested architecture (you have latitude here — this is a strong default, not a mandate)

- **Backend:** Node.js + Express + Socket.IO (or ws) for real-time game-state sync. Authoritative game state lives server-side; clients send intents (`acquireCard`, `rearrange`, `useFavor`, etc.), server validates against the rules engine and broadcasts the new state.
- **Rules engine:** a pure, framework-free state machine/module (no I/O) that takes `(state, action) -> newState` or throws on illegal moves. Keep this completely separate from networking and UI code — it's the part most likely to need tweaks later, and it's what the AI bots and any future automated tests will call directly.
- **AI bots:** implement as a module that, given the current state and a seat it controls, produces a legal action using reasonable heuristics (see "Rules the written doc left open" below for what "reasonable" should optimize toward). Doesn't need to be a strong player, just legal, non-stalling, and make sensible-looking decisions.
- **Frontend:** a single-page app (React+Vite is a good default) rendering each player's mat (8 slots), the central market/draft row, dice results, coins/hearts/stars/trophies, and the card images from `assets/cards/`. Card art is already fully illustrated and print-ready — just display the PNGs, no need to re-render card faces from scratch.
- **Room/session model:** a host creates a room and gets a shareable code/link; other players (or the host, for empty seats) can assign AI to any unfilled seat. 2-5 total seats per the rules.

## Full rules (verbatim from the "Current Rules" doc, v31)

### Equipment
- 5 Player mats — 8 slots each: slots 1-5 for Performers, slot 6 for Backdrop **or Trainer**, slot 7 for Prop **or Trainer**, slot 8 for Trainer only. (Updated per a later ruling — see "Multi-Trainer slots" below — a player can now have up to 3 Trainers active on their board at once.)
- 1 Central mat — draft cards, resource tokens, and a space indicating how many Tomato dice will be rolled that round.
- 10 dice total:
  - 5x D20 "Collection Dice" — faces: A, A, A, A, B, B, B, B, C, C, C, D, D, D, E, E, F, F, G, H (i.e. letters A-H with decreasing frequency A→H, matching the Letter printed on each of the 68 Performer cards). This was reversed from the original A→H increasing-frequency layout by a later ruling.
  - 9x D8 "Heckler"/Tomato dice — faces numbered 1-8 (matching player-mat slot numbers 1-8).
- 150 cards (updated from the original 139 per later card-set revisions; 145, then 144, immediately before the Sept 2026 revisions): 72 Performers (64 -> 68 -> 72; the "Troupe v34" revision of Sept 2 2026 reshuffled resource, power-dot, gender, letter and heart assignments wholesale, added 8 Coin performers and retired 4; a corrected sheet on Sept 3 fixed 33 letters, giving an exactly even 9 cards per letter A-H and 9 per (power dots, letter) pair), 10 Backdrop, 10 Prop, 18 Trainer (10 original + 11 added later, minus Atlas the Steadfast, Higgins the Pawnbroker, and Cassius the Second Act, all removed entirely — Amara the Reliquary was also removed for a time and later brought back under her original name with a reworded ability, so the net trainer count is 18), 10 Re-roll (Press Pass — **two copies each of 1/2/3/4/5**: the Sept 2026 revision cut 5, 6 and 7 back to 1/2/3/4, and a later ruling the same month brought **5** back as two copies, appended to the end of the deck so the already-printed sheets kept their layout; earlier revisions had one each of 1-7, and before that 2 copies each of only 1/2/3, and before that the Audience/"Tomato" re-roll cards too, all removed or reworked per later rulings), 14 Favor (7x "1st", 7x "2nd+"), 16 Resource (**Hearts 1/2/3/4/5 and Coins 1/2/3/4/5, one copy of each; Cards 1/2/3, two copies each** — the Sept 2026 revision replaced the old "two copies each of 1/2/3" for Hearts and Coins with a single copy running 1 to 5, and left the Card resources alone; the Star resource cards were removed from the game entirely).
- 163 tokens: 50 stars, 50 hearts, 50 coins, 13 trophies.
- 5 Draft Order stands (numbered 1-5).

### Object
Build the most legendary troupe of performers by earning Trophies. First to the trophy threshold wins (see Winning below).

### Setup
- Give each player a mat (5 Performer slots, 1 Backdrop, 1 Prop, 1 Trainer — all start empty).
- Shuffle the 148-card deck, place it centrally as the draw pile.
- Lay out a market of 4 cards next to the deck.
- Randomly pick a first player, give them Draft Order stand "1" and starting coins equal to 2x their stand number. Going clockwise, each next player gets the next stand number (stand 1 = 2 coins, stand 2 = 4 coins, stand 3 = 6 coins, stand 4 = 8 coins, stand 5 = 10 coins).

### Round structure
1. **Lay out the draft:** place face-up cards in the center equal to `(number of players x 2) + 1`.
2. **Draft phase:** players take turns strictly in current draft-order (stand 1 first, etc). On your turn you must do exactly one of:
   - **Acquire 1 card**, either:
     - free, from the face-up draft row, OR
     - from the 4-card market by paying its coin cost (market slots cost 1/2/3/4 coins left-to-right). The bought slot sits empty for the rest of the player's turn — prices do NOT shift or refill mid-turn, so a player buying several cards in one turn window (Maximillian the Magnate's extra market buys, or a chain of Favor bonus turns) sees every other slot at its original, stable price throughout. Only once that player's turn is genuinely over (play is about to pass to someone else) does the market collapse its sold gaps and refill from the deck back up to 4 cards.
     - Before deciding to acquire (or not), a player may reset the market by paying 1 coin — discard the current (up to 4) market cards, deal 4 new ones. This can be repeated any number of times (1 coin each) before the player's actual acquire decision for that specific turn, and it also immediately clears out any gap left by a sale on an earlier bonus turn in the same chain.
   - **Or, rearrange** your own board/reserve cards in any way (no new card gained) — this consumes the turn.
   - When a card is acquired: if it's a Performer/Backdrop/Prop/Trainer, place hearts on it up to its printed starting-heart value (pull heart tokens from the central supply). If its home slot on your mat is already occupied, you're offered a real choice — place it anyway (the old occupant moves to your reserve, unlimited size, includes draft picks you're holding) or send the newly acquired card to reserve instead, leaving the current occupant in place. A Trainer specifically tries slot 8 (Trainer-only) first, automatically, since it can never be occupied by anything else; only once slot 8 is already taken does it offer that same bump-or-reserve choice, among all 3 Trainer slots (6, 7, or 8). If the newly acquired card is a Resource or Favor card, see their special handling below instead of placing it on your mat.
   - Play continues around the draft-order stands, looping, until only 1 card remains face-up in the draft row — discard that last card, and the draft phase ends. (Players usually get ~2 turns/round, but can get more by spending Favor cards for extra turns, or fewer if they spent turns buying/rearranging instead of drafting.)
3. **Dice phase:**
   - **The instant the draft ends, a pre-roll Press Pass window opens** — see "Re-roll cards" below. Players still holding at least one Press Pass card are offered that decision **one at a time, in reverse draft order** — whoever took the round's *last* draft turn chooses first, then the seat before them in draft order, and so on around (owner's ruling, Sept 3 2026). The sequencing is the point: each player decides already knowing what everyone who drafted after them spent. Spend any number of your passes (a proactive click on the card, like a Favor, never forced), then confirm you're ready and the window passes on. The round's 5 shared Collection Dice do not start rolling until the whole queue is spent. Each spent card immediately rolls that many *private* Collection Die(s) — resolved exactly like a normal Collection Die, but crediting only the spender — in addition to, and independent of, the round's 5 shared dice below.
   - **Roll all 5 shared Collection Dice, one at a time, revealed live.** Each die is rolled, shown on screen, and left visible before the next one rolls — building up a row of 5 — so spectators can actually watch the round unfold; a live table of each player's resources earned so far this round updates as each die locks in. For each die result (a letter A-H) once locked, every player who has a Performer on their mat with that Letter collects: if that performer's printed resource is Star or Coin, place the token on the player's central token pool (not on the card); if it's a Heart, immediately assign it to any of that player's starter or reserve cards up to that card's max heart capacity.
   - **Assign the Trophy:** whoever earned the most Stars this round takes a Trophy token. Tie → most Coins overall (each tied player's total/career coin count, not just coins earned this round) wins it. Still tied → all tied players take a Trophy. The trophy winner(s) must then remove 1 heart from each of their "starters" (their 8 mat-slot cards — Performers, Backdrop, Prop, and Trainer — see "Rules the written doc left open" for why this includes non-Performer slots). Do not refill empty slots yet.
   - **Assign new Draft Order:** fewest Stars this round → stand "1" (goes first next round). Next fewest → stand "2", etc. Tie → fewer Coins currently owned (each tied player's total/career coin count, not just coins earned this round — same tie-break basis as the Trophy assignment above) gets the better (lower) stand; i.e. owning *more* coins right now is the worse draft pick on a stars tie. Still tied → fewer total Trophies gets the better stand. Still tied → roll-off.
   - **Roll Tomato dice, as one batch.** Roll a number of D8 Tomato dice equal to the current round number (round 1 = 1 die, round 2 = 2 dice, etc), capping at 7 dice from round 7 onward, all at once, and leave the whole batch visible before it locks. Between the roll and the lock, if the round's Mesmera the Veiled holder wants to, they may re-roll the entire batch once (see the Trainer table below). Once locked, for every rolled number 1-8, every player removes 1 heart from whatever currently occupies that slot number on their own mat. Every card also survives the hit that brings it to 0 printed hearts (per the owner's ruling) — reaching 0 hearts does not, by itself, discard a card. Only the *next* hit taken while a card is already at 0 hearts actually discards it. So a 1-heart card takes exactly 2 hits total to leave the stage, and a card that starts at 0 printed hearts discards on its very first hit. This applies to both Tomato-die hits and trophy-winner heart removal above.
   - **Review pause:** once both the 5 shared Collection Dice and the Tomato batch have fully resolved, every player gets a blocking prompt to look over the round's results (the earned-resources table, hearts lost, etc.) before continuing — a "Continue" click, required from every seat, with no other effect on the game (bots resolve theirs instantly; it only meaningfully pauses for a human).
   - **Refill:** any now-empty mat slots (from tomato hits or trophy heart removal that emptied a card) are refilled from that player's reserve, if they have a suitable card there.
4. **Next round:** lay out `(players x 2) + 1` new draft cards, add 1 more Tomato die to next round's roll, and repeat from step 2.

### Winning
First to reach the Trophy threshold wins, based on player count: 2 players → 6 Trophies. 3 players → 5 Trophies. 4 players → 4 Trophies. 5 players → 3 Trophies. Simultaneous qualifiers win together.

### Card-type handling
- **Performers** (68): the core troupe cards — Characteristic (Graceful/Powerful/Dramatic/Haunting), Type (Singer/Dancer/Acrobat/Illusionist), Gender, Letter (A-H, drives Collection Die matches), Resource (Star/Heart/Coin), starting Hearts (0-2, `startingHearts` in `card_database.json`), heart capacity (1-3, `maxHearts` — the total heart pips printed at the bottom of the card, filled + empty; NOT always equal to `startingHearts`, so a performer frequently starts below its own max and can receive more hearts later), and Power Dots (1-4, currently flavor/unused by any written rule — surface it in the UI but it has no mechanical effect unless you find one implied elsewhere). Both `startingHearts` and `maxHearts` were re-derived directly from the printed card art (counting filled vs. empty heart icons per card) after the original data proved stale relative to the final regenerated card images.
- **Backdrop/Prop** (10 unique effects each, one copy of each = 20 cards): occupy the Backdrop or Prop mat slot. See `card_database.json` for each card's `boostKind` (characteristic or type) and `boosts` (which value(s) it matches — the 2 wildcard cards per set match all 4 characteristics or all 4 types). Every Backdrop/Prop, solid-match or wildcard alike, has `startingHearts: 2` and `maxHearts: 2` — it's acquired already at full hearts and has no room for more (a Heart resource card or Collection Die heart payout simply has nowhere to place a heart on it; those hearts get routed to another card with room, or forfeited if nothing has room). Effect (see "Rules the written doc left open" below for sourcing): when a Collection Die causes a Performer on the same player's board to collect, and that Performer's characteristic/type matches the player's equipped Backdrop or Prop, that performer collects +1 extra unit of their printed resource. **Per the owner's clarification, the 2 wildcard cards per set (the "Any Characteristic"/"Any Type" ones, printed with all 4 initials — G/P/D/H or S/D/A/I) only activate at all once the player has at least one active Performer (one of their 5 Performer slots) covering every one of those 4 values; until then they're dormant and grant no boost to anyone, even a Performer that would otherwise match one of the 4 listed values.** The 8 single-match cards (`boosts` list of length 1) have no such gate — they work immediately, as before. Implemented in `engine.js`'s `hasFullSet()`/`boostCount()`.
- **Trainer** (19 unique, 1 copy each): occupy slot 6, 7, or 8 (a player may have up to 3 active Trainers at once — see "Multi-Trainer slots" below), 3 starting hearts, grants a unique passive/activatable ability — see the table below.

### Multi-Trainer slots

A later ruling changed slots 6 and 7 from single-purpose (Backdrop-only / Prop-only) to dual-purpose: slot 6 holds a Backdrop *or* a Trainer, slot 7 holds a Prop *or* a Trainer, slot 8 remains Trainer-only. This means a player can have 0-3 Trainers active simultaneously, at the cost of giving up their Backdrop and/or Prop slot(s) to do it. `trainerActive(state, seat, trainerId)` checks all 3 slots; nearly every Trainer ability keys off this one helper, so this single change is what enables stacking Trainers. Acquiring a Trainer card tries slot 8 first, automatically (it can only ever hold a Trainer, so there's nothing to weigh); only once slot 8 is already occupied does the player get a prompt, offering a choice of any of the 3 Trainer slots (6, 7, or 8 — bumping whichever one they pick to reserve) or sending the newly acquired Trainer to reserve instead (same bump-or-reserve UX as every other full-slot acquisition).
- **Resource cards** (24 = 12 unique x2): never occupy a mat slot. On acquire, immediately resolve per `card_database.json`'s `effect` field (take coins / assign hearts / gain stars / draw cards — see assumptions below for the "Cards" resource) and discard the card.
- **Favor cards** (16 = 2 unique x8): go to reserve on acquire. Timing, per the owner's exact ruling: a "1st" Favor is usable on the player's first turn of the round *or any later turn that round* — it never expires once eligible; a "2nd" Favor is usable starting on their second turn or any later turn that round, but not their very first (i.e. `favorEligibleNow(triggerAfterTurn, turnsSoFar) = turnsSoFar >= triggerAfterTurn - 1`, where `p.turns` is turns already completed this round). While eligible, the card can be discarded from reserve at any point during one of your own turns, before you take that turn's main action, for an extra turn right then. A player may spend more than one eligible Favor this way — either several queued up before the current turn's main action (each queues its own extra turn, taken in order once that action resolves), or one at a time across turns: spend a Favor, take the bonus turn it grants (a fresh pre-action window, with its own `mainDone` reset), and from there decide again whether to spend another Favor or just take that turn's normal action. There is never a forced prompt for this — the owner was explicit that the game must never ask "do you want to use a Favor?"; the card just becomes clickable in the player's own reserve whenever eligible, and not clicking it simply means play continues as normal (`useFavor` action, separate from the turn's main action). Rule: you can't spend a Favor draft pick to draft another Favor of the exact same timing (1st-for-1st or 2nd-for-2nd) — i.e. a bonus turn granted by a Favor may not be used to draft another same-timing Favor; normal acquisition (paying coins or drafting on a normal turn) is unaffected.
- **Re-roll cards** (10 = Press Pass 1/2/3/4/5, two copies of each; Press Pass 6 and 7 and the Audience/"Tomato" re-roll cards were removed from the game per later rulings): go to reserve on acquire. The instant the draft phase ends (before the round's 5 shared Collection Dice roll at all), the players still holding at least one Press Pass card get a pending pre-roll window **one seat at a time, in reverse draft order** — the round's last drafter first, then backwards through draft order (owner's ruling, Sept 3 2026): discard any number of them from reserve — a proactive click, never a forced choice, same UX as a Favor — then confirm they're done and the window moves to the next seat. Each discarded card immediately rolls that many *private* Collection Die(s) of the spender's own (N = the card's printed number; Delphine Silvertongue doubles it) — resolved exactly like a normal Collection Die, but only the spender collects from them — in addition to, not instead of, the round's 5 shared dice. The shared dice don't start rolling until every seat in the queue has closed its window; a seat that gave away or spent its last pass before its turn came round is skipped silently. A player with no Press Pass cards gets no window at all; if nobody holds one, the shared dice roll immediately, same as before Press Pass existed.

Curio's automatic roll stayed letter-gated (only Acrobats matching the rolled letter benefit) rather than becoming "all your Acrobats regardless of letter."

## The active-performer rule

Whenever a Trainer's ability keys off "your performers", it means only the ones **on stage** — mat slots 1-5. Cards in reserve are held, not performing, and never count. Every performer-counting ability routes through `activePerformers()` / `countActivePerformers()` so the rule can't drift apart card by card (Tomasso's Dancers, Ezra's Illusionist, Barnaby's Graceful, Stainglass's Dramatic, Valentino's Powerful, Jonas's Haunting, Curio's Acrobats, and the wildcard Prop/Backdrop full-set gate).

There are exactly **two exceptions**, both written into the Trainer's own printed text:

- **Bellacanto the Choirmistress** — her Singers *in reserve* are treated as active Performers in every respect but one (owner's ruling, Sept 3 2026). They collect on a matching Collection Die; they complete the 4-of-a-kind set that switches on a wildcard "Any Characteristic"/"Any Type" Prop or Backdrop; and they count for Barnaby's Graceful discount, Tomasso's Dancer dice, Ezra's Illusionist check, Jonas's Haunting cash-in (he can discard one straight out of reserve), Valentino's Powerful re-roll allowance and Stainglass's Dramatic draw. **The sole exception: they cannot lose hearts at the end of a round.** That needs no special case in code — `heartHit()` addresses cards by mat slot index, so nothing in reserve is reachable by trophy fatigue or a Tomato die. Everything routes through `activePerformers()`, which is the single definition of "performing"; `hasFullSet()` goes through it too, which is what makes a reserve Singer able to complete a set.
- **Amara the Reliquary** — she may move hearts on *any* of her cards, reserve included.
Every other Trainer follows the rule without exception, including **Jonas Quickfinger** (only stage Haunting performers can be cashed in) and **The Vanishing Valentino** (only stage Powerful performers grant Tomato re-rolls).

## Prop/Backdrop boosts apply from any mat slot

`boostCount` scans all 8 mat slots for Prop/Backdrop cards, not just the natural Backdrop (6) and Prop (7) pair. Madame Barre can place an acquired card in any slot, and a Prop or Backdrop sitting in a non-traditional slot is still equipped and still boosts. (Reserve is still excluded — a card in reserve isn't in play.) This was a bug: previously such a card silently granted nothing.

## Acquired cards get their hearts wherever they land

A card sent straight to reserve on acquisition is just as "acquired" as one placed on the mat, and arrives with its printed starting hearts filled in (or its maximum under Madame Coeur) — see `setStartingHearts`, shared by `placeInSlot` and both send-to-reserve paths. Previously reserved acquisitions got no hearts at all and arrived empty, which mattered the moment they were promoted into a slot.

## Reserve is overflow only — a card that can be a starter must be one

Reserve exists to hold cards with nowhere to go, not as a parking space. Whenever the engine settles during the draft phase, any reserve card that legally fits an *empty* mat slot is moved into it (`enforceReservePlacement`). The owner's canonical example: acquiring a Prop displaces the Trainer sitting in the Prop/Trainer slot, and if the Trainer-only slot is open, that Trainer must relocate there rather than idling in reserve.

- **Forced moves happen silently; genuine choices prompt.** A card with exactly one legal empty slot is just moved (there is nothing to decide). A card with several — a Trainer with slots 6, 7 and 8 all open — raises the existing mandatory `refill` prompt, which already refuses any resolution that leaves a fillable slot empty.
- **Madame Barre is the sole exception**, as her whole standing rule is that her holder *may* park acquired cards in reserve with a matching slot open. The invariant is skipped entirely for a seat while she's active, and `relocateReserveOnBarreLeaving` sweeps up the instant she leaves play. Her exemption also covers the **end-of-round refill**: her holder *may*, but is never required to, fill empty slots from reserve at the end of a round (every other seat must fill every slot it can).
- **Not applied during the dice phase**, which keeps its own designated `refill` stage *after* trophy fatigue and the Tomato batch resolve — so a card promoted out of reserve never takes hits from dice that were already rolled this round. That stage enforces the same "fill every slot you can" rule, so the invariant still holds by the end of every round.

## Token supply (90 hearts / 30 stars / 80 coins)

The physical component counts are tracked so the app can flag a supply that's too small for real play (`TOKEN_SUPPLY`, `tokenSupply()`). Counts are **derived** from board state rather than incremented at each grant and spend, so they can never drift: coins on players' mats, hearts currently sitting on cards, and stars earned *this round* (`roundStars` — `p.stars` is a lifetime tally, not physical tokens). This means tokens cycle back to the supply exactly as they would on a table: coins the moment they're spent, hearts the moment they're lost or their card is discarded, stars at the end of each round once the Trophy is decided.

**Alert-only by design** — a depleted pool never blocks a gain, it just gets flagged (log line, toast, and a standing banner). The goal is validating component counts, not making scarcity a game mechanic. `state.supplyAlerts` records each pool that has run dry and its worst deficit, and deliberately persists for the rest of the game rather than clearing when the pool recovers.

**Heart count history:** hearts were originally specced at 80, but this tracking immediately showed simulated 5-player games peaking at ~86 hearts in play — a genuine shortage at high player counts. The supply was raised to **90**, which clears the peak.

**Measured peaks** (40 simulated AI games per player count, after the raise to 90 hearts):

| Players | Hearts | Stars | Coins |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 36 / 90 | 11 / 30 | 36 / 80 |
| 3 | 51 / 90 | 13 / 30 | 65 / 80 |
| 4 | 67 / 90 | 20 / 30 | **81 / 80** |
| 5 | 84 / 90 | 22 / 30 | **85 / 80** |

> ⚠ **Coins are now the binding constraint.** At 4-5 players the coin pool overran 80 in 5 of 80 simulated games (peak 85). Hearts and stars have comfortable headroom. Raising coins to ~95-100 would give the same margin hearts now have — flagged rather than changed, since the component count is the owner's call. Note these are AI games; human players hoard coins differently, so treat the peaks as indicative rather than exact.

## Rules the written doc left open — documented assumptions

The source rules document is a work-in-progress design doc, not a complete spec, and is silent on a few things this build needs. Here's what to implement, and why, so the owner can quickly correct anything that's off:

1. **Prop/Backdrop mechanical effect** — the owner specified directly: *"+1 collection of resource when rolled performer matches prop or background."* Implemented as: when a Collection Die causes one of your Performers to collect, and that Performer's characteristic or type matches your equipped Prop/Backdrop's boost, you collect one extra unit of that performer's resource. Clarified further by a later ruling: the 2 wildcard "Any Characteristic"/"Any Type" cards only activate once the player has at least one active Performer of every one of the 4 values they nominally cover — otherwise dormant, granting no boost at all.
2. **"Card" resource cards' effect** — confirmed by the owner: drawn cards go into the player's starting slots (the same slot a normal acquisition would fill), not straight to reserve. Implemented as: each drawn card is placed into an empty slot matching its type (Performer/Backdrop/Prop/Trainer) if one is open; if every matching starting slot is already full, the player is prompted to choose between placing it anyway (bumping the current occupant to reserve, same as a normal acquisition) or sending the drawn card to reserve instead. Non-slottable draws (Resource/Favor/Re-roll cards) resolve or reserve exactly as they would from a normal acquisition.
3. **"Starters" scope for trophy-winner heart removal and tomato-die targeting** — the doc says "remove 1 heart from each of their starters" and "remove hearts from the indicated slots" (slot numbers 1-8). Since Backdrop/Prop/Trainer cards are printed with hearts too, this build treats all 8 mat slots (5 Performers + Backdrop + Prop + Trainer) as valid "starter" targets for both the trophy-winner heart removal and Tomato die slot-hits, not just the 5 Performer slots.
4. **How many Collection Dice are rolled per round** — the doc says "roll collection dice one at a time" without stating a count. Implemented as: all 5 Collection Dice are rolled once per round (not per player), each resolved fully (every player checks for a match) before moving to the next die.
5. **Re-roll card numbers (1-5)** — superseded four times now (see the "Re-roll cards" entry above for the current mechanic): first by a ruling where the printed number was the size of a shared re-roll pool usable on any of the round's 5 Collection Dice, with priority when more than one player held a Press Pass; then by a ruling that removed Press Pass 4 and 5 from the deck (three copies each of 1/2/3, 9 cards total) and turned each card into a proactive, Favor-like spend usable at any point during the draft phase, rolling that many *private* Collection Die(s) for the spender alone; then by a ruling which (a) restricted that spend to a dedicated pre-roll window that opens only once the draft phase ends and closes right before the round's 5 shared dice start — not usable earlier in the draft — and (b) trimmed the deck further to two copies each of 1/2/3 (6 cards total); then by a ruling which expanded the set back out to one copy each of 1 through 7 (7 cards total); then (Sept 2026) by a ruling which cut 5, 6 and 7 again and restored two copies each of the survivors — two each of Press Pass 1/2/3/4, 8 cards total; and finally (Sept 3 2026) by a ruling adding **two copies of Press Pass 5** back — **two each of Press Pass 1/2/3/4/5, 10 cards total**, 150 in the full deck as of the latest revision. The two new cards were appended to the *end* of the deck order rather than sorted into the press-pass block, so pages already printed and cut keep exactly the cards they had (`TAIL_IDS` in `Pipeline Scripts/print_sheets.py`). The Audience/"Tomato" re-roll cards were removed from the game entirely, and Mesmera the Veiled's ability was changed to a one-time re-roll of the whole Tomato batch rather than adding extra re-roll attempts to these cards; Delphine Silvertongue's multiplier on a spent Press Pass's roll count was also later changed from tripling to doubling.

Please flag any of these 5 in the README as "assumptions made — confirm with Travis" so they're easy to find and correct later.

## Stars earned after a round is scored carry over

A round's stars decide the Trophy and are then wiped when the next round starts. But stars can still arrive *after* that comparison — most obviously Jonas Quickfinger firing on performers the Tomato dice discard, which resolve after both the Trophy and the new draft order are settled. Those stars would otherwise be earned and instantly deleted.

Every star gain routes through `addStars()`, which banks anything earned once `state.dice.trophyAssigned` is set into `p.carryStars`; `startNextRound` then opens the new round with `roundStars = carryStars` instead of 0. The lifetime `p.stars` tally is unaffected either way. Draft order is unaffected too, since it's assigned before the Tomato dice roll.

## AI valuation notes (measured, not guessed)

Two calibrations worth recording, both derived from ~1,900 sampled bot decision points in real simulated games rather than intuition:

- **A "draw N cards" Resource is worth N acquisitions, not N coins.** A drawn card is placed exactly like a drafted one, so its value should track a board card, not a token. Measured: a random deck card scores **~3.8**, while the *best* card in a typical draft row scores **~4.1** — a single random draw is very nearly as good as a free draft pick. The old `0.9 * N + 0.5` curve priced Draw-3 at **3.2**, roughly 29% of its true ~11 value, so the bot passed it over for almost anything. Now `drawValue(N)` sums `DRAW_CARD_VALUE (3.8)` with a `DRAW_DECAY (0.96)` per extra card, reflecting the measured diminishing return (the cards arrive together, so later ones more often find the empty slots already taken). Bots now take an offered Draw-3 about **92%** of the time. `DRAW_CARD_VALUE` is calibrated excluding the Card resources themselves, so the estimate can't inflate its own input.
- **A Press Pass is worth its printed number times what your board collects, times what a collected unit is worth.** It buys `count` private Collection Die rolls (doubled by Delphine), so a flat score is wrong on every axis at once — it ignores the printed number (1 to 5 in the current deck), the holder's board, *and* what a collected resource unit is actually worth. `pressPassValue()` prices them from `expectedUnitsPerCollectionRoll()`, which mirrors the real payout exactly (boosts, Orsino's G/H bonus, Bellacanto's reserve Singers, letter frequency), so value is strictly proportional to the printed number — a Press Pass 5 is always worth exactly five times a Press Pass 1 to the same seat, and a higher card can never rank below a lower one. Two corrections, both measured. **(1) Timing:** a Press Pass isn't spent until the pre-roll window at the *end* of the round's draft, by which point the holder has filled more of their stage — over 240 AI-only games (5,107 decision points), units/roll rises from a mean **0.523** at draft time to **0.675** at spend time. `PRESS_PASS_EMPTY_SLOT_UNITS` (0.08) credits each empty Performer slot with its share of that gap, which makes the projection unbiased (0.674 predicted vs 0.675 actual); the old flat `PRESS_PASS_MIN_UNITS` floor was biased ~11% low and left a half-full stage underpriced, and now survives only as a 0.25 guard for a stage with nothing on it. **(2) What a unit is worth — the real problem:** a unit was priced at **0.9**, the same as a Coin resource card, but ~41% of collected units are **Stars** (the trophy currency the bot already prices above Coins everywhere else), they land at the end of the round exactly when round stars are compared for the Trophy, and the card costs no mat slot to cash in. Swept head-to-head against the previous bot, win share climbs monotonically from 50.0% at 0.9 to ~53% around 1.5-1.7 and falls off again by 2.4; `PRESS_PASS_UNIT_SCORE` is now **1.5**. Press Pass 7 pick rate went from 18% to 37%, Press Pass 1 from 4.4% to 3.8% — the bot now takes the big ones and still leaves the small ones.
- **A Favor is an extra card out of a fixed pool, and spending it is a timing decision.** A round deals exactly `2 x players + 1` cards and ends with 1 left, so the draft pool is fixed — a bonus turn is not a replacement pick, it is an *extra* one, taken out of an opponent's share. Spent before the main action, it grants a bonus turn immediately afterwards, so what it really buys is **the two best cards in the row back to back**, before any opponent gets at the second one. **Valuation:** measured over 240 AI-only games (16,821 draft decisions), the card a bot takes averages **4.31** and the next-best card still in the row averages **3.34**, so a Favor spent at a random moment is worth about the mean of the two (~3.8). Held until the right window, the card the bonus turn takes averages **5.85** instead. `FAVOR_FIRST` (**4.7**) is that number discounted for the delay and for the risk of never getting a good window; `FAVOR_SECOND` is **3.6**. Swept head-to-head, win share rises through 4.4-5.0 and then falls off a cliff by 6.4 (49.8%) and 7.4 (45.3%), where the bot starts drafting Favors over the very cards they exist to help it take. A "1st" is usable from turn 1 of any round; a "2nd" only from turn 2, and measured across real games **6.7% of a player's rounds never reach a second turn at all**, stranding it — hence the gap between the two. **Spending (`wantsFavorNow`):** the bot used to spend a Favor reflexively on the first turn it was eligible. That policy spent 1,534 Favors of which **27% bought a bonus turn that never happened** — `finishTurn` ends the draft the instant the row drops to its last card, and that check runs *before* the queued bonus turn is granted, so a Favor spent with fewer than 3 cards in the row is silently thrown away — and **54% bought a second pick scoring under 3.0**. Mean value of the card the bonus turn took: **2.78**. It now spends only when (a) the row still holds 3+ cards, counting one per bonus turn already queued, and (b) the card its bonus turn would take scores at least `FAVOR_SPEND_MIN` (**4.5**, swept — it beats 3.5, 4.0 and 5.0 on every seed set). Result: **0% wasted, 0% junk, mean value of the card taken 5.69**, and the "1st" Favor pick rate went from 9% to 26%. The high draft value only works *because* spending is selective — under the reflex policy the same sweep punished anything above 4.0. The one case where a bot still correctly takes the "2nd" Favor is on a Favor bonus turn of matching timing, where drafting another same-timing Favor is illegal by rule.
- **Resetting the market is a real option.** Bots previously never paid the 1 coin. They now reset when the best draft-row option *and* the best affordable market option both score under `BOT_WEAK_OPTION` (3.0 — genuinely below the ~3.8 average card) and they hold at least `BOT_RESET_MIN_COINS` (5), so they can still afford whatever turns up. Capped at `BOT_MAX_RESETS` (2) per turn: resetting doesn't consume the main action, so an uncapped version would loop forever against a persistently weak board. Observed in play on ~11.5% of turns.

## AI bot behavior guidance

### Coin economics, and why the bot still banks coins

Travis reported an AI seat finishing a game with 29 unspent coins. It is real, and
it is **not** what was costing the AI games. Measured over ~28,000 mirrored A/B
games across five independent seed sets, every lever swept — cheaper coins when
rich, a lower market premium, more market resets, valuing Coin resource cards
down when rich, and every combination — came back win-rate neutral or worse. One
configuration measured **+1.5pp on its own tuning seed set and exactly 50.0% on
hold-out seeds**: a textbook overfit, and the reason nothing in `bot.js` is ever
hand-picked.

The cause is structural. A coin buys only from a 4-slot market, and buying costs
the same main action a **free** draft pick costs, so the market has to be clearly
better to be worth taking; and career coins are the Trophy tiebreaker, so a bank
is not worthless. Coins simply arrive faster than the board can absorb them.

What did ship is a behaviour fix, not a strength claim (`COIN_SPEND_SURPLUS` 16,
`COIN_SPEND_FLOOR` 0.45, `RICH_COINS` 10, `RICH_MARKET_PREMIUM` 0.2): past a bank
of 10 the bot stops demanding that a market card *clearly* beat the free pick and
settles for beating it at all, and past 16 the marginal coin is priced below face
value. That cuts mean unspent coins from ~9.3 to ~8.7 and trims the long tail, at
a measured cost of nothing (50.1% over the same 28,000 games). **Do not revert
these because they "don't help" — they are not there to help; and equally, do not
treat them as validated strength gains.** The floor matters: a bot that treats
coins as free empties itself, cannot afford a market reset, and measured 46.4% —
the worst result of the entire sweep.

Separately, and unrelated to hoarding: a **Heart resource is now worth what fits,
not what is printed**. Hearts with nowhere to go are forfeited outright, so a
5-heart card on a board with room for 2 delivers 2. Flat `0.9 x amount` was
harmless while the biggest Heart card was a 3; the Sept 2026 deck runs to 5.


Doesn't need to be optimal, just legal and coherent. Reasonable heuristics: prefer free draft picks that fill an empty mat slot over ones that would bump a card to reserve; weight Performers by matching resource type to whichever resource the bot is behind on; "Card" resources (draw N) are valued a bit above their linear face value once N is 2 or 3, since each drawn card is effectively a free extra acquisition (the Star resource cards were removed from the game entirely, so there's no round-competitiveness valuation needed for resource cards anymore); spend Favor cards for extra turns when the row is actually worth doubling up on rather than on the first eligible turn (see the Favor valuation note above; it may still spend more than one eligible Favor before its main action in the same turn window, counting each queued bonus turn against the cards left in the row); likewise, once its pre-roll Press Pass window opens (see "Re-roll cards"), spend any Press Pass card(s) it's holding whenever it has at least one Performer on board (little downside — a private die roll or two is close to free value), biggest printed number first so a window cut short by a takeover or disconnect has still banked the most rolls, then close the window; use Mesmera's Tomato-batch re-roll only when the current batch threatens one of the bot's own cards, and close her window explicitly when it declines (Valentino now waits on her, so leaving it open would hold his shut); with Valentino, re-roll exactly the dice that threaten one of the bot's own cards, most dangerous first, up to its Powerful-performer allowance — and decline the window outright when nothing on the table hurts it, which is why his selective re-roll is the safer of the two; when a full slot forces a bump-or-reserve choice (Performer row, a Backdrop/Prop's single slot, or a Trainer once slot 8 is taken), prefer an empty allowed slot if one exists, otherwise bump whichever occupant is least valuable rather than sending the new card to reserve; once both this round's Collection Dice and Tomato dice have resolved, acknowledge the review pause immediately (nothing to weigh — it only meaningfully pauses for a human); otherwise acquire the highest-value card available for free before considering a market purchase. The engine itself resolves bot decisions instantly (no artificial "thinking" delay) so games never stall — but per the owner's request, the server paces the *broadcast* of a bot's moves: 2 seconds after a bot fully completes a turn before the next bot acts, and (during the dice phase) each Collection Die and the Tomato batch is held on screen briefly — long enough for any bot's Mesmera reaction to resolve first — before locking in and moving to the next step, so human spectators/players can actually watch the round unfold. The one exception to that timer: if a connected human (not a bot) still owes a decision on the open Tomato batch — Mesmera the Veiled's re-roll first, then The Vanishing Valentino's selective one once hers is closed — the server skips the timer entirely and just waits for their action (`mesmeraRerollTomato` / `valentinoRerollTomato` / `keepTomatoRoll`), so they're never rushed into losing a decision window to the clock. Bot seats are resolved before that check, so anything still open at that point belongs to a person.

### Sound and card-reading conventions (client)

- **Two sounds, not one.** A turn ending and *your* turn starting are different events and only one needs you to look up. Every completed turn plays a short, quiet, dry click for everyone — the table equivalent of hearing a card go down. The seat that is now up gets a two-note rising beep on top of it. Both are synthesised with Web Audio (no asset file), primed on the first click because browsers block audio before a gesture, and muted together by the header toggle.
- **Hover-to-enlarge is Trainers only, after a 1 second dwell.** Trainers are the only cards carrying a paragraph of ability text that genuinely cannot be read at mat size; zooming everything turned the board into a minefield of things leaping about under the cursor, and a short delay was not enough to stop it.
- **Every one of an opponent's cards — mat and reserve alike — reports its hearts as red heart tokens laid across the middle of the card**, not as a "2/3" pill. A count of tokens is read at a glance from across a table; a fraction at 44px is not. Their reserve cards render at the same size as the cards on their mat, and nothing is boxed over the art, so the card's own printed face still reads. Your OWN cards keep the pill, because there the remaining capacity is the number that matters — you are the one filling it.
- **No card carries a letter chip.** An earlier revision stamped the Collection letter over the corner of reserve cards and repeated it beside the reserve toggle; both were removed at Travis's request. The letter is printed on the card's own bottom-right medallion.
- **Drawn cards are shown, not named.** A "draw N" Resource puts the actual card faces in front of the player: the placement prompt renders every card that draw produced, with the one currently asking for a home outlined and the rest dimmed, and the slot/reserve choice underneath. If every drawn card found its own slot and nothing prompted, a `drawnCardsReveal` pause shows them once anyway — otherwise cards would appear on the mat unannounced and the player would never see what they drew. The batch rides along on the pending as `data.drawn` + `data.source`; Ezra's leftover and Stainglass's keep are single cards from no batch and carry neither.
- **Token sizes are absolute px per card size class, never a percentage.** `font-size: 30%` resolves against the inherited 14px body font, not the card width, so it silently rendered ~4px tokens. `test/client.test.js` asserts the rule stays in px and at least 12px; the xs numbers are set so three tokens (the deck maximum) plus gaps and strokes measure 42.4px inside a 44px card.
